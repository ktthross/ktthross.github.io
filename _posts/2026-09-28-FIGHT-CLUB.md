I've had some thoughts about Fight Club (the movie) kicking around in my brain for a long time. It was absolutely one
of my favorite movies when I was younger. I haven't rewatched it in a while and likely won't rewatch it soon, but I
gotta put these thoughts into the universe, just in case someone else needs to read them.

One thing that always stood out to me in the movie is the protagonist's refrigerator. The first time we see inside the refrigerator, he is waxing on about how consumerism has replaced his identity:

```
I would flip through catalogs and wonder, "What kind of dining set defines me as a person?"  We used to read pornography.  Now it was the Horchow Collection.

...

I had it all.  Even the glass dishes with tiny bubbles and imperfections, proof they were crafted by the honest, simple, hard-working indigenous peoples of wherever.
```

In this scene, he opens the refrigerator door to reveal that there is nothing inside but condiments, toppings and dressings. Then later, we see the refrigerator lying on the ground, broken and busted apart from the explosion in his condo. Again we see the contents exposed and lying out for everyone to see. What intrigued me is that, at this moment, the voice-over characterizes this as embarrassing.

I never understood why our protagonist would take this moment to call out how embarrassing it was to have his empty refrigerator exposed. Surely no one there is looking at the contents and coming up with a value judgement about him. And even so, why is a refrigerator full of condiments a sore point? We really see how disconnected he is from reality, and how caught up in his own delusions he is, before we know anything about his descent into Tyler.

I see the refrigerator as a proxy for his internal state. When you open him up, there is nothing of substance. There is nothing nourishing, delicious, flavorful, or even junk food. It shows how hollow he is, and what is in there are accessories to food. All the things in his life are accessories to a life, but ultimately he is just as empty as the refrigerator.

The other thing that really stands out to me in the movie is the death of Robert Paulson. Robert stands out, not simply because of his large breasts, but because he is an exception to the rules and the spirit of Fight Club and Project Mayhem.

- He breaks the first two rules of Fight Club by talking with the narrator in the street.
- He wears a shirt during fights, which is explicitly forbidden.
- When getting tested for admission into Project Mayhem, he is rejected, as all candidates are. True candidates should stand there for three days and nights (I think it's three) to prove their worth, and are explicitly not encouraged; in fact, they are abused and belittled. When Robert is told "You're too old, fat man. And your tits are too big," he begins to walk away. It is the narrator who goes out to encourage him and tell him how to work the system to get into Project Mayhem.
- Project Mayhem is a "boys only" sort of club. Tyler even waxes that another woman is the last thing we need. Robert's secondary sexual characteristics directly challenge the ideas about masculinity that are core to the project.

There may be other examples, but it is very clear that Robert is the fly in the ointment. He is antithetical to the thesis of Project Mayhem. He is soft, emotional, has great big tits, and doesn't fit in with the rest of the all-singing, all-dancing crap. He is the opposite of Marla, who is a woman but acts more like a member of Project Mayhem. She is despondent and hopeless in the way that the members are, and she is a threat. Not only is she the catalyst for destroying the narrator's cozy worldview at the therapy sessions, but they also kidnap her and try to take her away. Robert is the biological male presenting as female, and Marla is the biological female presenting as male. They are threats.

I suspect this analysis is well-trodden territory. I don't follow any sort of scholarship on the movie, but I needed to establish this for my next point. Project Mayhem murdered Robert Paulson.

In the movie, the viewer is presented with the death of Robert through the presentation of his body and a story told by a member of Project Mayhem. Here is the relevant section of the script:

```
                        MECHANIC
                       (out of breath)
                 We were on assignment...

     EXT. SCULPTURE PARK - NIGHT (FLASHBACK)

     A SCULPTURE adorned with a giant GLOBE on top.

                             MECHANIC (V.O.)
                 We were supposed to kill two birds
                 with one stone:

     A SERIES of EXPLOSIONS blasts the GLOBE free.  It ROLLS...

                             MECHANIC (V.O.)
                 A piece of corporate art...

     The GLOBE ROLLS downhill, to the street -- rolling over one
     parked LUXURY CAR after another, crunching car roofs and
     causing windows to explode...

                             MECHANIC (V.O.)
                 ... and trash a trendy coffee bar.

     Then, the GLOBE arrives at the lobby of a HOTEL...
     BROADSIDES a limo, RICOCHETS... ROLLS directly into the
     front of a closed ARROSTO coffee bar, SMASHING windows...
     DECIMATING coffee push-pats...

     Bob, the Mechanic and Ricky FLEE, LAUGHING at their

                 We had it all worked out, man.  It

     The Mechanic looks up from Bob's corpse.

                             MECHANIC
                 They shot Bob... they shot him in the
```

Let's examine this story more closely and see what they are actually asking us to believe.

1. They went to vandalize some corporate art and trash a coffee shop.
2. They were seen by the police.
3. They started to run away.
4. The police shot at them and Robert was killed.
5. They picked up the body and continued to flee.
6. They made it back to Project Mayhem headquarters with the body.

How did they get the body back? It's stated earlier in the film that Robert was a bodybuilder, and even in the scene, it shows several Project Mayhem members struggling to lift his body to lay it on the table. The members of Project Mayhem are fit and muscular, so they should be better than average at something like lifting a body. Are we to believe that the members of Project Mayhem, while being pursued and shot at, somehow rallied, collected the body of a bodybuilder, and outran the police?

The omission of the details of their escape is perfect. We don't question how they got back. Robert was not killed by the police; he was killed by Project Mayhem, and they covered it up because Robert was a threat to the project.
